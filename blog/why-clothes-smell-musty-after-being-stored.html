Why Do Clothes Smell After Being Stored? Causes and the Fix

Storage changes the environment around fabric. Heat, humidity, low airflow and traces of soil or odor can turn a clean-looking garment into a musty one.

Quick answer

Clothes can smell musty after storage because fabric kept in a closed, humid environment has little airflow and can retain moisture and volatile compounds. If a garment went into storage even slightly damp or carried residual odor, the enclosed wardrobe can make the smell much more obvious.

“Clean” and “odor-free” are not identical. A garment may be visually clean while still carrying low levels of odorants, skin oils or detergent residue. Inside a closed cupboard, those compounds have nowhere to disperse.

Does storing clothes slightly damp really matter?

Yes. Moisture is the first thing to eliminate. The US Environmental Protection Agency advises keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent, and notes that damp materials dried within 24–48 hours usually do not grow mould. A closed almirah in the monsoon can stay above that line for weeks.

So never pack clothing while cuffs, collars or seams are still damp. One damp item changes the small environment inside a closed storage space and passes its smell to the fabric around it.

Why do winter clothes smell after months in storage?

Seasonal garments spend a long time compressed with limited ventilation. Dust, skin residue, perfumes and household odors accumulate. When the wardrobe is opened months later and the fabric warms up, the trapped volatile compounds become easy to notice.

Fibre type matters too. A 2024 study that exposed six fibres to an odorous synthetic sweat solution found cotton and viscose released less of the ketones and aldehydes than wool, nylon and polyester at 30 minutes, and by 24 hours polyester was releasing the most. A stored polyester jacket can hold yesterday's smell longer than a cotton shirt beside it.

How do you freshen stored clothes without washing everything?

Work in this order: air, inspect, wash what needs washing, then treat what is left. Reversing it, spraying first, only hides a damp problem.

  1. Empty the storage space. Take the garments out and leave the box, suitcase or almirah open so it airs as well. This separates the garment's smell from the cupboard's.
  2. Inspect each piece. Look for damp spots, visible mould, stains and obvious soil. A fabric spray is not a mould remover.
  3. Air the garments fully. Hang or spread them so air reaches folded seams, cuffs, collars and inside layers. Removing trapped moisture is usually the fastest fix.
  4. Decide what needs washing. Wash anything dirty, sweat-marked or mouldy. A clean garment that only smells stored does not need a full wash; overwashing structured clothes wears them out.
  5. Treat the residual smell. On clean, completely dry clothing, mist the inside of the collar, underarms and back from 15–20 cm, let it dry for 15–30 seconds, then fold or hang it.

When should you actually wash the garment?

Wash it when there is visible soil, sweat, body-oil buildup, stains or a smell that is still there after a day of airing. Odor treatment is a between-wash tool, not a replacement for laundering when the garment needs laundering.

Storage reset

Air the space. Dry the garment. Then treat residual odor. Do not use fragrance to hide a damp storage problem.

How do you store clothes so the musty smell does not come back?

Keep moisture and old smells out of the cupboard before the next season.

  • Store only fully dry, clean clothes. A shirt worn once and put away carries sweat and skin oil that turn stale over months.
  • Do not overpack. Space between garments lets air move; a tightly packed shelf stays humid.
  • Open and air the almirah on dry days, and wipe any damp corners before refilling it.
  • Skip strong mothballs if you hate the smell: naphthalene clings to fabric. See getting mothball smell out of clothes.
  • If freshly washed clothes still smell once they are put away, the cupboard may be the source: see why clean clothes smell in the wardrobe.

Sources: US EPA, A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home · Textile sorption and release of odorous volatile organic compounds from a synthetic sweat solution (2024).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can clothes become smelly just from being stored?

Yes. A closed, humid space traps moisture and volatile compounds on fabric that were barely noticeable when the clothes went in.

Do I need to wash every stored garment?

No. Wash what is dirty, sweat-marked or mouldy. Clothes that are clean but smell stored usually need airing and a light fabric odor treatment, not a full wash.

Should I put stored clothes in the sun?

A few hours of airing in dry, moving air helps. Keep dark and delicate fabrics out of long, harsh sun so the colour does not fade.

Can a fabric odor spray fix stored-clothing smell?

It can clear residual odor on a clean, dry garment. Visible mould or heavy contamination needs proper cleaning or replacement.

Can I use perfume on old clothes?

Perfume adds a scent on top. It does not remove a stale or musty smell, and the two together often smell worse.
