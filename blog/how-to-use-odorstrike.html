How to Use ODORSTRIKE

Most complaints about fabric sprays are application problems. Three zones, the right distance, and ten seconds of patience solve nearly all of them.

Quick answer

How do you use ODORSTRIKE?

Hold the bottle 15–20 cm from the fabric and mist three zones: the inside collar, both underarm panels and the upper back between the shoulder blades. That is 4–5 sprays for a shirt, 5–6 for a jacket or hoodie. Keep your hand moving and use light passes rather than soaking one spot. Let it air-dry about 10 seconds, then wear. One application gives up to 8 hours of odor protection on fabric; reapply after a workout or a humid commute. It goes on the garment, never on skin.

"I sprayed it once and it did not work" is the most common complaint about every fabric mist ever sold, and almost every time it turns out to be one of three things: sprayed the wrong part of the garment, sprayed from too close, or did not wait the ten seconds. None of those is a formula problem.

Here is the whole ODORSTRIKE routine, in the order you would actually do it.

How many sprays per garment?

Four to five for a shirt. Five to six for a jacket or hoodie. The product works on contact, so even coverage of the right zones matters far more than volume.

The three zones that actually hold odor

Inside collar, underarm panels, upper back between the shoulder blades. Those three carry nearly all of it, and everything else on a shirt is close to irrelevant.

The logic is skin contact plus trapped heat. The collar sits against the neck all day and picks up the most transferred sebum. The underarm is where the apocrine glands are, so it gets the lipid-rich sweat that bacteria turn into the sharpest compounds. The upper back is where a bag, a chair or a car seat holds fabric against a warm body with no airflow.

The chest, the sleeves and the outer front of a shirt get almost none of this. Spraying them uses product and buys nothing.

Collar first

If you only have time for two sprays, put both inside the collar. Conversation happens at about a metre with your collar at the other person's nose height — it is the zone that decides how you read.

Distance, technique and dry time

Fifteen to twenty centimetres, hand moving, two or three light passes, then about ten seconds to dry.

Holding the nozzle closer than 15 cm turns the mist into droplets. Droplets soak a patch, and a soaked patch dries slower than the fabric around it, which is what produces a temporary ring. That ring is the single most common reason people think a fabric spray stained something — the mechanics of it are in the staining guide.

The fabric should feel barely cool to the back of your hand, not damp. Then give it the ten seconds. The isopropyl alcohol in the carrier is what makes that dry time possible: it drives the actives into the fibre and evaporates, leaving HPβCD and Zinc PCA where they need to be. Put the shirt on while it is still wet and you interrupt that.

Before wear or mid-day reset?

Both, and they do different jobs.

Before wear is the primary application, and it is prevention. Spraying a clean or part-worn garment before you put it on means HPβCD is already in the fibre when the first odor compounds form, so they get trapped as they appear rather than after they have built up. This is the application that gets you the full window.

Mid-day reset is correction, and it is a thirty-second job. Collar and underarms, two or three sprays, ten seconds. Worth doing after lunch on a hot day, before a meeting, or around hour four of a long event.

For gym kit there is a third moment: spray the tee after the session, before it goes into the bag. A damp synthetic sealed in a bag for six hours is the ideal environment for the bacteria you are trying to limit. Treating it on the way out of the gym is far more effective than treating it when you get home.

When to reapply

One application gives up to 8 hours of odor protection on fabric. The variables that shorten it are activity, ambient heat and humidity.

An air-conditioned desk day sits at the top of that range and often does not need a top-up at all. A humid Indian commute followed by a training session sits well below it. The practical rule: if you have sweated properly since you last sprayed, reapply to the collar and underarms.

On cost, the arithmetic is straightforward. A 50ml bottle gives approximately 250 sprays. At 2–3 sprays per use that is roughly 80 to 125 shirt-fresh uses, which is about ₹0.92 per spray and around ₹2–3 per use at the ₹229 launch price. Most people on daily use get four to six weeks from a bottle.

What ODORSTRIKE does not do

Three limits worth being explicit about, because knowing them is what stops the product disappointing you.

It is not a deodorant. It goes on fabric, never on skin. Your skin still needs whatever you normally use — the two are solving different halves of the same problem, which deodorant vs fabric mist covers properly.

It is not a wash replacement. A visibly dirty or heavily soaked garment needs laundering. And a synthetic that has been building up residue for months needs a stripping wash — a mist prevents odor developing on a clean garment, it does not dissolve established biofilm out of one that is not.

It is fabric only. Not skin, not hair, not a room spray. On silk, zari and heavy embroidery, spot-test a hidden seam first and let it dry fully before you commit — the festive wear guide has the fabric-by-fabric breakdown.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many sprays of ODORSTRIKE should I use?

Four to five sprays for a shirt or t-shirt: two on the inside collar, one on each underarm panel, one on the upper back between the shoulder blades. Five to six for a jacket, hoodie or blazer because there is more fabric and an inner lining. More than that is wasted — the product works on contact, so coverage matters and quantity does not.

Where exactly should I spray on a shirt?

Three zones hold nearly all the odor: the inside of the collar, the underarm panels, and the upper back between the shoulder blades. Those are the areas with the most direct skin contact and the most trapped heat. Spraying the chest, sleeves or the outer front of a shirt achieves very little.

How far away should I hold the bottle?

Fifteen to twenty centimetres, with your hand moving through the pass. Closer than that and the spray lands as droplets rather than a mist, which soaks one patch and can leave a temporary water ring while the rest of the fabric dries faster. Two or three light passes beat one heavy one.

How long does ODORSTRIKE last on a shirt?

Up to 8 hours of odor protection on fabric per application. Activity, heat and humidity shorten that — an air-conditioned desk day sits at the top of the range, a humid commute plus a gym session does not. Reapply to the collar and underarm zones when the day runs long.

How many uses do you get from a 50ml bottle?

A 50ml bottle gives approximately 250 sprays. At 2–3 sprays per use that is roughly 80 to 125 shirt-fresh uses, which works out at about ₹0.92 per spray and around ₹2–3 per use at the ₹229 launch price. Most people on daily use get four to six weeks from a bottle.
