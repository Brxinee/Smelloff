Rented Sherwani or Lehenga Smells? What to Do

Renting a sherwani, a lehenga or a chaniya choli makes sense for an outfit you will wear once. The catch is that someone else wore it before you. Here is how to check it, air it and wear it so neither you nor your deposit suffers.

Quick answer

How do you freshen a rented sherwani or lehenga?

Ask the shop when it was last cleaned, smell the underarms and collar before you accept it, and hang it inside out in shade with a fan for a few hours at home. Wear your own clean cotton layer under it: a vest and your own kurta or churidar under a sherwani, your own blouse or a cotton slip where the shop allows. Read the care label before spraying anything; never spray a dry-clean-only outfit.

Rental outfits have changed Indian weddings: a sherwani, a Jodhpuri suit or a bridal-looking lehenga for a fraction of the cost of buying one. You pick it up a day or two before the function, wear it once and return it. What you do in those two days decides whether it feels fresh on you.

Is it safe to wear a rented outfit?

A properly cleaned rental from a reputable shop is fine; your own layer underneath is what protects you.

Bacteria do survive on fabric. In a 2000 study in the Journal of Clinical Microbiology, common skin bacteria survived on hospital fabrics for days, and staphylococci lasted from 1 to 56 days on polyester. That is a reason to ask about cleaning and to wear your own clean cotton layer against the skin, not a reason to panic. Shops that clean between rentals will tell you how they do it.

What should you check when you pick it up?

Smell it, look at it, and ask about it before you sign.

  • Smell the underarms, collar and waistband, where the last wearer's sweat would be.
  • Ask when and how it was cleaned, and whether you may air or steam it.
  • Check for stains, loose work and missing buttons, and point them out so they are noted against the deposit.
  • Read the care label so you know what you may and may not do to it.

How do you air a rented outfit before the function?

Out of the cover, inside out, in shade, with moving air.

Take it out of its plastic cover as soon as you get home. Hang it on a broad hanger inside out, near an open window or under a fan, for a few hours or overnight. Keep it out of direct sun and away from the kitchen. If the shop allows, a light steam from the inside helps creases and freshens the lining.

What should you wear under a rented sherwani or lehenga?

Your own clean cotton, as much of it as the outfit allows.

  • Under a sherwani: a cotton vest, and your own kurta and churidar if the set allows.
  • Under a lehenga: your own blouse if you can, or a thin cotton slip under the skirt.
  • Under a chaniya choli: a cotton vest or slip under the choli. Our guide to keeping a chaniya choli fresh for garba covers nine nights of it.

This layer takes your sweat, so the rented piece stays fresher for you and for the next person.

Can you use a fabric spray on a rented outfit?

On your own layer, yes. On the rented piece, only if the shop and the care label allow it.

Many rented sherwanis and lehengas are dry clean only, and spraying them could affect your deposit. The simple answer is to treat what you own: mist the underarms and collar of your vest or kurta with a clothing deodorant for the layer under your outfit from 15–20 cm, and let it dry for 15–30 seconds before dressing. If you do treat a washable rental lining with permission, patch-test a hidden seam first, and never spray zari or heavy embroidery.

How do you return it without a smell dispute?

Air it the night after, and return it dry. Hang it inside out under a fan as soon as you take it off, so sweat and smoke do not settle into it overnight in a bag. Return it on time, in its cover, folded the way it came. For outfits you own and will wear again, see our festive wear odour guide.

Honest limits

Airing and your own cotton layer make a rental fresher for you; they do not clean it. If a rental smells strongly of someone else's sweat or of damp at pickup, ask for another piece. Never spray a dry-clean-only outfit.

Sources

Neely, A. N. and Maley, M. P. Survival of enterococci and staphylococci on hospital fabrics and plastic. Journal of Clinical Microbiology, 2000. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I wash a rented sherwani?

No. Most rented ethnic wear is dry clean only, and washing it could damage the work and your deposit. Air it and wear your own layer underneath.

How long before the function should I pick it up?

A day or two before gives you time to air it and get any fitting fixed.

Can I spray perfume on a rented outfit?

Perfume oils can mark silk and light fabrics. Put fragrance on your skin before you dress, not on the rented piece.

What if it smells bad when I collect it?

Ask for another piece or for it to be cleaned. Point out the smell before you sign for it.
