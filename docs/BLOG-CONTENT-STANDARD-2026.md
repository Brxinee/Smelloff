# Smelloff Blog Content Standard — 2026

This is the editorial contract for every public Smelloff guide. The goal is not to manufacture pages for keywords. The goal is to publish pages that a customer would genuinely bookmark, a journalist could quote, and a search/answer system can understand without guessing.

## 1. The ranking target

A Smelloff guide should be useful on its own. Search optimization is the packaging around useful information, not the reason the page exists.

Every guide must have:

- One primary search intent and a clearly different angle from nearby Smelloff posts.
- An answer-first opening. The reader should know the answer to the title question within the first 1–2 paragraphs.
- A descriptive title tag, one visible H1, a useful meta description, and a self-referencing canonical.
- A strong page introduction that sounds written by a person who has actually dealt with the problem.
- Short paragraphs, descriptive H2/H3 headings, practical examples, and a clear next action.
- Internal links that help the reader continue the topic, not links added just to increase link count.
- At least one useful original element: a decision rule, test, comparison table, checklist, founder observation, product-use example, or clearly explained interpretation of research.
- Sources for non-obvious scientific, technical, legal, medical, statistical, or product-comparison claims.

There is no target word count that guarantees rankings. Long-form is a requirement only when the topic genuinely needs depth. As a house standard, pillar/commercial guides should normally land around 1,800–2,800 words and focused support articles around 1,200–2,000 words.

## 2. SEO

Use natural variants of the primary query in the H1, opening, one or more useful subheadings, and body copy where they belong. Do not repeat a keyword just to hit a density target.

Prefer specific search intent over broad stuffing. For example:

- "how to remove sweat smell from a shirt without washing" is a problem to solve.
- "fabric odor spray" is a category term.
- "ODORSTRIKE" is a brand/entity term.

A page can rank for many related phrases when the page actually covers the topic well. Do not create near-duplicate articles for every wording of the same query.

## 3. AEO / answer extraction

Write sentences that can survive being quoted out of context.

The first answer block should:

1. State the conclusion directly.
2. Explain the main reason in plain language.
3. Add the important limitation, when one exists.

Use tables, definitions, numbered steps, and FAQs only when they make the answer easier to consume. FAQ structured data is not a ranking lever for an ordinary commercial site; visible usefulness matters more than markup volume.

## 4. GEO / LLM discoverability

Make the Smelloff entity unambiguous and consistent:

- Brand: Smelloff
- Product: ODORSTRIKE
- Size: 50ml
- Category: fabric-only odor-control mist
- Use: clothing fabric only
- Location: Hyderabad, India

Do not blur "fabric-only" into generic deodorant, perfume, body spray, room spray, or upholstery spray language.

Use quotable factual statements where useful. Put important facts in visible HTML as well as structured data or `llms.txt`. Never rely on hidden metadata as the only place a fact exists.

## 5. Citations and evidence

Use primary or high-authority sources whenever possible: PubMed/PMC, peer-reviewed journals, official ingredient/product documentation, government sources, standards bodies, and first-party manufacturer documentation for product facts.

The evidence hierarchy is:

1. First-party Smelloff product facts for Smelloff-specific claims.
2. Original scientific papers for scientific/mechanism claims.
3. Official manufacturer documentation for competitor/product facts.
4. High-quality secondary sources only when a primary source is unavailable.

Do not cite a generic search result as evidence. Link to the underlying source.

Never claim that an external study "proved ODORSTRIKE works" unless the study actually tested ODORSTRIKE. Distinguish clearly between:

- what the research shows about textiles/odor chemistry;
- what Smelloff's formula contains;
- what Smelloff claims from product testing;
- what the author observed personally.

## 6. Current product truth — mandatory

The current public formula is **v3.1** with an 11-ingredient INCI list. The hero actives are **HPβCD (hydroxypropyl beta-cyclodextrin)** and **Zinc PCA**.

Do not publish old formulation details such as:

- "Zinc ricinoleate"
- "Zinc PCA 1.5%"
- "β-cyclodextrin 1.5%"
- "86% distilled water"
- "5% IPA"
- "1% fragrance"

unless the founder has explicitly re-confirmed those numbers for the current public formula.

Current customer-facing facts are 50ml, ₹229 prepaid UPI with free shipping, ₹289 COD including ₹60 handling, about 250 sprays, and approximately 10 seconds air-dry when used as directed. The product is clothing-only and is not a replacement for washing visibly dirty or heavily soiled garments.

## 7. Human voice

Avoid:

- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "Whether you're..."
- generic three-part conclusions
- repetitive "Here's why" sections every few paragraphs
- fake certainty
- claims that sound like an ad written around a keyword
- fabricated tests, customer counts, or performance numbers

Prefer concrete scenes: the shirt that smells after the commute, the gym tee that comes back from the wash still weird, the jacket worn twice during a wedding, the cupboard opened after a humid week.

The founder voice can be used when it adds genuine first-hand context. Do not turn every guide into an origin story.

## 8. Recommended article anatomy

1. H1 that matches the actual question.
2. 1–2 paragraph direct answer.
3. "What is actually happening?" or equivalent explanation.
4. Practical diagnostic section.
5. Solution / decision rule / step-by-step section.
6. Important exceptions and limitations.
7. Smelloff/ODORSTRIKE section only where naturally relevant.
8. Sources & further reading.
9. 3–5 concise FAQs when they add genuine value.
10. Related guides + one relevant product CTA.

## 9. Publishing gate

A post is not ready when it merely has a title, keywords, schema and 1,000 words. It is ready when:

- the answer is obvious quickly;
- the page explains the mechanism without pretending to be a science paper;
- claims can be traced to evidence or clearly labeled as Smelloff claims/observations;
- the product facts match the current truth source;
- the page is differentiated from existing Smelloff articles;
- the reader can actually do something useful after reading it.

## 10. Research-before-writing rule

For every new or materially revised article, research current external sources before drafting. Re-check the evidence when the topic touches a changing product specification, competitor, price, regulation, platform guidance, or recent study.

The research notes should record the source, what it supports, and the exact limitation of the evidence. Research is part of production, not a final proofreading step.
